## Why Nellis AFB Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most of the housing stock around the base, whether it's an older ranch-style home, a manufactured home, or a rental built to serve base personnel, sits on a concrete slab with no crawlspace to vent moisture. When a supply line fails or a water heater lets go, the water doesn't pool in a basement where you'll notice it. It spreads laterally under flooring, tile, and baseboards, often undetected until carpet pads feel spongy or laminate starts to cup. Add in the valley's monsoon pattern from July through September, when storms can overwhelm roof drainage and push water under door thresholds and through roof penetrations, and you've got two very different water-loss patterns that call for different responses. Swamp cooler lines, common on older homes in this part of the valley that predate widespread central AC, are another frequent culprit; a cracked line or clogged drain pan can leak for days behind a wall before anyone notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Nellis AFB

The first thing our IICRC WRT-trained techs do on a slab-foundation home is map moisture under the flooring, not just at the surface, because slab assemblies hold and transfer water differently than a wood subfloor over a crawlspace. We pull standing water with truck-mount extraction, then set containment and drying equipment calibrated to the valley's naturally low humidity. That last part matters more here than almost anywhere else we work: in a desert climate, it's easy to over-dry framing and finish materials if equipment isn't monitored closely, which can cause its own cracking and warping. We follow IICRC S500 structural drying guidelines and track moisture content daily until the materials read dry, not just until the air feels dry, before we close out a job.

## Local Note

One thing that catches people off guard out here: the caliche-heavy soil common across the valley doesn't absorb water the way loamier soil does, so after a hard monsoon storm, water can sit against a foundation or in a yard for hours or days with nowhere to drain. That standing water finds its way in through grade-level gaps, stucco weep screeds, and door thresholds long after the rain has stopped, which is why we always check exterior grading and drainage patterns as part of a water damage assessment near Nellis AFB, not just the interior source.

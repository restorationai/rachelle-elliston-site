That construction style changes how a leak behaves. There's no crawlspace to catch a slow drip and no basement to flood first, which means water from a failed supply line or water heater often spreads laterally across the slab before anyone notices a stain or a soft spot in the flooring. By the time carpet pad feels spongy or baseboards start to bow, the moisture has usually been traveling for days.

## Why Summerlin South Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Las Vegas valley's hard water is rough on the copper and PEX supply lines common in homes this age, and mineral scale buildup is a frequent contributor to pinhole leaks and fitting failures we see on service calls in this part of the valley. Add the monsoon pattern that hits Southern Nevada from July through September, when short, intense downpours can overwhelm desert landscaping and roof drains faster than the ground can absorb them, and you get two very different water intrusion patterns in the same neighborhood: slow interior leaks from plumbing, and sudden exterior intrusion from storm runoff pooling against stucco walls and patio doors.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Summerlin South

On slab construction, moisture mapping has to happen before any demolition starts, because water trapped between the slab and finished flooring doesn't always show up on a surface-level visual check. Our crews use moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace how far water has migrated under tile, laminate, or carpet before pulling anything up. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water and saturated pad or carpet so drying equipment isn't fighting a losing battle. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to target the slab itself, not just the air in the room, since concrete holds moisture longer than most homeowners expect. We log readings daily until the materials are back to a dry standard, not just dry to the touch.

Las Vegas summers push air conditioners into near-constant operation, and that nonstop recirculation is exactly how smoke, pet, and fire odor spreads from one room into every return vent in the house. Whether the source is a kitchen fire, a long history of indoor smoking, or lingering odor after a water loss dried out, Desert Valley Contracting pairs ozone, hydroxyl, and thermal fogging methods to break down odor at the molecular level rather than mask it, and we calibrate the approach to how this city's building stock and climate actually behave.

## Why Las Vegas Properties See Odor Removal Issues

Much of the Las Vegas valley was built out fast during the 1990s and 2000s boom, which means a large share of the housing stock is stucco-over-block or stucco-over-frame construction with tight building envelopes designed to keep conditioned air in and desert heat out. That same tight envelope traps odor. Smoke from a stovetop fire, cigarette residue built up over years, or must left behind by a slow leak doesn't have anywhere to vent, so it settles into drywall, insulation, and HVAC ductwork and keeps circulating every time the system kicks on. Add in the valley's fine desert dust, which works its way into return vents and mixes with smoke or soot residue into a gritty film that plain cleaning doesn't fully lift, and odor jobs here often need more HVAC-focused work than the same job would in a more humid climate.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Las Vegas

We start by identifying what's actually causing the odor and where it has migrated, since porous materials like insulation, subfloor, and cabinetry absorb odor compounds differently than sealed drywall or tile. From there we typically combine thermal fogging to penetrate the same gaps the odor used to get into materials, with hydroxyl generators for occupied spaces where people or pets are present, since hydroxyl treatment doesn't require the same evacuation that ozone does. Ozone treatment gets reserved for vacant properties or sealed-off work areas where we can control exposure and ventilate fully before anyone re-enters. Because Las Vegas runs dry for most of the year, odor-causing residues can bind tightly to surfaces rather than staying airborne, so we sometimes introduce controlled humidity ahead of treatment to loosen residue the desert air has baked into porous materials.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Our crews carry ozone generators, hydroxyl units, and ULV thermal fogging equipment, and we also address HVAC systems directly rather than just treating the rooms odor is noticed in. In a valley where central air runs most of the year, an odor job that skips duct cleaning and coil treatment usually relapses within weeks as residue trapped in the ductwork keeps reintroducing smell. We document before-and-after air quality observations and, on fire-related jobs, coordinate with our IICRC FSRT-trained technicians so odor work lines up with any structural fire and smoke restoration already underway.

## Las Vegas Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many Las Vegas neighborhoods fall under HOA-managed master-planned communities, and some associations have rules about exterior equipment staging, generator noise, or work hours that we confirm with the property manager before setup rather than after. On the insurance side, odor remediation tied to a covered fire or water loss typically gets documented alongside the main restoration claim, and we photograph and log the treatment so it supports the adjuster's file rather than arriving as a surprise line item.

## Local Note

One thing crews learn fast working odor jobs in this valley: Las Vegas's low humidity means ozone and hydroxyl treatments can finish faster on surface-level odor than they would in a humid climate, but deeper residue baked into stucco, block, or attic insulation by years of 110-degree summers needs more dwell time, not less, because the odor compounds are more tightly bound to the material. Rushing the back half of a job here based on how fast the air smells clean is the most common mistake we see from DIY attempts.

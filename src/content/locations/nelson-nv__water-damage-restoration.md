## Why Nelson Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Nelson's building stock dates back to the area's mining-era history, and even newer construction out here tends to be remote, low-slung, and built close to natural drainage paths carved by decades of desert washouts. When summer monsoon cells drop heavy rain over Eldorado Canyon in a short window, dry washes near a property can carry more water than the surrounding grade is built to shed, and that water finds the path of least resistance, often straight into a crawlspace or slab edge. Add in well-and-septic infrastructure that's common this far from municipal utilities, and a plumbing failure or supply line break can sit undetected longer than it would in a home tied into city water with a meter that flags unusual usage. Dry desert air helps some, but it also means materials that look dry on the surface can still be holding moisture well below.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Nelson

We start with extraction, pulling standing water off flooring and out from under cabinetry and baseboards before it has a chance to wick further into framing. From there we set containment and begin structural drying following the IICRC S500 standard, using moisture meters to track what's happening inside walls and subfloor, not just what's visible. Because many Nelson properties are seasonal or weekend-use, we pay close attention to materials that may have been sitting with a slow leak for longer than anyone realized, older plumbing runs, galvanized pipe, or well-pressure tank connections that have started to fail gradually rather than all at once. Our crews carry IICRC Water Damage Restoration (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) training, so the drying plan is built around the specific materials in your home, not a generic timeline.

## What flood damage restoration actually involves

Flood damage restoration covers the extraction, drying, and sanitizing of a structure after water enters from an outside source, monsoon runoff, a breached retention basin, an overflowing wash, or municipal drainage backing up during a storm. Unlike a clean supply-line leak, floodwater is almost always Category 2 or 3, meaning it's carried soil, debris, or contaminants from outside the home. That changes the scope: porous materials that touched floodwater (carpet pad, baseboard, drywall below the waterline) typically can't be dried in place and have to come out. Crews use submersible pumps and truck-mount extractors for standing water, then move to low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers and axial air movers once the bulk water is gone. On a typical single-story North Las Vegas home with a few inches of floodwater, extraction takes a few hours and structural drying runs several days, tracked daily with moisture meters against dry standard readings.

## Our process

1. **Safety and source control.** Before anyone steps inside, we confirm the electrical panel is safe to approach and identify whether water is still entering the structure.
2. **Category classification and extraction.** We classify the water source, then pull standing water with pumps and truck-mounted extractors, prioritizing rooms where water is migrating into adjacent areas.
3. **Contaminated material removal.** Flood water categorization usually means carpet, pad, and affected baseboard or drywall get removed rather than dried, following IICRC S500 guidance.
4. **Structural drying and monitoring.** Dehumidifiers and air movers are set based on room size and material type, with moisture readings logged daily until materials hit dry standard.
5. **Cleaning and antimicrobial treatment.** Affected surfaces are cleaned and treated to address contamination risk before reconstruction begins.

## What separates a good flood response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating a flood like a clean water leak: drying carpet and pad that should have been removed, or skipping behind baseboard and inside wall cavities where water wicks upward. Adjusters increasingly ask for daily moisture logs and photos documenting Category classification, so documentation gaps can slow a claim even when the drying itself was done correctly. Another frequent miss is under-sizing equipment for the square footage, which stretches a three-day dry-out into a two-week job and invites secondary mold growth. A thorough response also checks HVAC returns and ductwork near the flood zone, since these pull contaminated air through the system if not isolated early.

## What does flood damage restoration cost?

Costs vary with how much water entered, how long it sat before extraction started, and how many materials were contaminated versus simply wet. ## Seasonal & regional considerations

North Las Vegas sees its highest flood risk during monsoon season, roughly July through September, when short, intense storms overwhelm washes and street drainage faster than the desert soil can absorb it. Homes near natural washes or with grading that slopes toward the foundation are especially exposed. Outside monsoon season, flood calls more often trace back to irrigation line breaks or retention basin overflow after an unusually wet winter storm system.

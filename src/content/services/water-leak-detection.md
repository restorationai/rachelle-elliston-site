A water bill that jumps without an obvious reason, a warm spot on the slab, the faint sound of running water when every fixture in the house is off: these are the signals that send homeowners searching for leak detection instead of waiting for a wet ceiling to tell them what's wrong. Hidden leaks behind drywall or under a concrete foundation can run for weeks before they surface, and by then the damage is no longer a plumbing fix, it's a restoration job. Pinpointing the source before that happens is the whole point of this service.

## What water leak detection actually involves

Leak detection is diagnostic work, not demolition. The goal is to locate a water source precisely enough that a plumber or restoration crew can open only the area that needs it, instead of tearing out an entire wall or slab on a guess. Technicians use acoustic listening equipment to pick up the sound of water moving through pipe under concrete or behind finished surfaces, thermal imaging cameras to spot temperature differences that indicate moisture, and moisture meters to confirm readings once a likely area is identified. For slab leaks specifically, pressure testing on the plumbing system helps confirm whether the leak is on the supply side or the drain line before any cutting happens. Most inspections take one to three hours depending on the size of the home and how many fixtures and lines need to be checked.

## Our process

1. **Interview and symptom review.** We start by asking what prompted the call, a high water bill, a damp patch on carpet, a sound under the floor, because that history narrows down which lines to test first.
2. **Non-invasive scanning.** Acoustic sensors and thermal imaging cover the suspect area without opening walls or flooring, so most of the diagnostic work happens before anything gets cut.
3. **Pressure and isolation testing.** Shutting off sections of the plumbing system one at a time helps confirm whether the leak is in a supply line, a drain line, or an irrigation line, which changes who handles the repair.
4. **Point-of-leak confirmation.** Once the location is narrowed down, we mark the exact spot and depth, so any necessary access cut is small and targeted rather than exploratory.
5. **Written findings.** The homeowner gets a report describing the leak location, suspected cause, and whether secondary moisture or early mold growth was found during the inspection, which matters for both repair planning and insurance documentation.

## What separates a good leak detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake is guessing based on where water is surfacing rather than where it's entering. Water travels along the path of least resistance, so a wet spot on a wall or floor is often several feet from the actual leak, especially with slab leaks where water can track along a vapor barrier before rising. A less careful operator cuts open the visible wet area, finds nothing, and starts opening more drywall. A thorough inspection isolates plumbing zones and confirms the leak acoustically or thermally before anyone picks up a saw.

The second mistake is stopping at "leak found" without checking how long it's been active. If moisture has been present for more than 24 to 48 hours, there's a real chance of hidden mold growth inside wall cavities or under flooring, and that needs to be flagged and addressed, not just the plumbing issue. Insurance adjusters reviewing a claim will also want documentation of when the leak was discovered and whether it was sudden or gradual, since that distinction affects coverage.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

North Las Vegas sits on soil with a notable clay and caliche content, and ground movement from seasonal temperature swings can put stress on slab plumbing over time, which is part of why slab leaks show up more often in older desert-built homes than in newer construction with updated piping materials. Summer heat also causes expansion and contraction in exposed or shallow-buried lines, particularly around pool equipment and irrigation systems, which is a common source of leak detection calls between late spring and early fall. Because the valley gets very little rainfall, a wet spot in the yard or a damp patch inside the home is rarely weather-related, which usually points straight back to a plumbing source.

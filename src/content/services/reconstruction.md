When the drying equipment comes out and the dust settles, a property is often left with exposed studs, missing drywall, a kitchen down to subfloor, or a roofline open to the sky. That gap between mitigation and move-back-in is where reconstruction work happens, and it's where a lot of homeowners get stuck: who pulls the permit, who frames the wall back, who makes sure the finish matches what was there before the loss. Desert Valley Contracting Inc handles that full rebuild, from framing to final paint, under one scope.

## What reconstruction services actually involve

Post-damage reconstruction covers everything that happens after demolition and drying: framing repairs, subfloor replacement, drywall and insulation, electrical and plumbing rough-in coordination, flooring, cabinetry, trim, and paint. For a fire-damaged room that might mean replacing charred studs and roof decking before any finish work starts. For a water loss it might mean pulling baseboards and lower drywall that stayed wet too long, then rebuilding from the studs up. Timeline depends heavily on permitting and material lead times more than labor itself, a single bathroom rebuild typically runs two to four weeks once permits clear, while structural work on framing or roof trusses can run longer. Desert Valley Contracting Inc holds Nevada contractor's license #0072652-B & B6, which covers the building and specialty trade categories this kind of work requires.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and estimate.** We document every damaged assembly, wall, floor, ceiling, and write a line-item scope that separates mitigation already completed from reconstruction still needed.
2. **Permitting and code review.** North Las Vegas requires permits for structural framing, electrical, and plumbing rough-in on most rebuilds. We pull what's required before any framing starts.
3. **Structural framing and rough-in coordination.** Studs, joists, and sheathing get replaced where fire, water, or impact compromised them, and we coordinate electrical and plumbing trades before walls close.
4. **Insulation, drywall, and finish work.** Once rough-in passes inspection, we insulate, hang and tape drywall, then move into flooring, cabinetry, trim, and paint.
5. **Final walkthrough and punch list.** We walk the space with you, note anything that needs adjustment, and close out permits with the final inspection.

## What separates a good reconstruction response from a bad one

The most common mistake is closing up walls before framing has fully dried or before an adjuster has had a chance to see the full extent of structural damage. Drywall hides problems, and once it's hung, proving hidden rot or char to a carrier gets harder. A second mistake is skipping permits on work that technically requires one, which can surface later during a home sale or insurance claim and force a homeowner to open walls back up. Adjusters generally look for photo documentation of removed materials, a clear line between what mitigation covered and what reconstruction covers, and confirmation that code-required upgrades, like GFCI outlets or updated egress, were addressed rather than skipped to save cost. A reconstruction contractor who's also IICRC-certified in water, fire, and structural drying tends to catch these handoff issues because they understand both sides of the job.

Costs vary widely because every structural reconstruction project depends on square footage, the trades involved, and how much of the structure needs rebuilding versus finishing. ## Seasonal & regional considerations

North Las Vegas summers push triple-digit heat, which speeds up drywall mud and paint curing but also means materials left staged outdoors can warp or off-gas faster than expected, scheduling has to account for that. Monsoon season brings flash flooding and wind-driven damage in short, intense bursts, which tends to spike demand for framing and roof reconstruction in late summer. Winters here rarely freeze hard, but older homes in the valley with exposed or poorly insulated plumbing runs can still see a burst-pipe rebuild after a rare cold snap.

Smoke that's seeped into drywall, a pet accident that's soaked into subflooring, or a sealed-up vacation home that smells like mildew the moment you open the door: these are odor problems that air fresheners and open windows don't solve, because the source isn't in the air. It's embedded in porous materials, trapped in HVAC ductwork, or baked into finishes by heat. Desert Valley Contracting Inc treats odor as a material-level problem, not a fragrance problem, which is the difference between a smell that goes away and one that comes back in July when the AC kicks on.

## What Odor Removal and Deodorization actually involves

Odor molecules cling to drywall, insulation, subfloor, carpet pad, and HVAC components in ways that surface cleaning can't reach. A single-room pet odor job might be resolved in a day. A whole-house job following a kitchen fire or a long-term water intrusion, where smoke or moisture has traveled through wall cavities and duct runs, typically runs two to five days depending on how much material has to be treated or removed.

The toolkit matters here. Ozone generators oxidize odor molecules at the chemical level and work well in unoccupied spaces with a controlled off-gas period. Hydroxyl generators do similar chemistry more slowly but safely in occupied homes, which matters when a family can't relocate during the work. Thermal fogging vaporizes a deodorizing agent into a fine mist that follows the same path smoke took, reaching into cracks and cavities that a sprayer never touches. Which equipment we use, and in what combination, depends on the source material, the square footage, and whether people or pets are staying on-site.

## Our process

1. **Source identification.** We trace the odor to its actual material source, whether that's scorched framing behind a wall, saturated pad under carpet, or biofilm inside duct runs, instead of just treating the room that smells worst.
2. **Material assessment and removal.** Porous materials that have absorbed odor past the point of practical treatment (charred wood, soaked insulation, odor-saturated pad) get removed. Treating what should be replaced wastes time and rarely holds.
3. **Thermal fogging or ozone treatment.** Depending on occupancy and material type, we fog a deodorizing agent to mirror the odor's original path, or run ozone treatment in a sealed, unoccupied space on a timed cycle.
4. **Hydroxyl deodorization for occupied spaces.** Where residents or pets need to stay in the home, hydroxyl generators run continuously at safe exposure levels, breaking down odor compounds without evacuating the house.
5. **Verification pass.** We recheck the space, including HVAC supply and return runs, after the treatment cycle to confirm the odor hasn't just been masked temporarily by the equipment still running.

## What separates a good odor removal response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating the symptom instead of the source, running an ozone machine for a few hours and calling it done while the actual saturated material stays in place. The smell returns within days, usually when humidity rises or the HVAC cycles on and redistributes trapped particles through the ducts.

Adjusters reviewing odor claims look for documentation that ties the treatment to the source: photos of the removed material, moisture or char readings where relevant, and a record of what equipment ran for how long. A job log that just says "deodorized" without specifying method and duration is a red flag on a claim file. We document source findings, removed materials, and equipment run times as part of every scope, which matters if a carrier questions the extent of work.

Duct contamination is also frequently skipped. If smoke or odor has been circulating for any length of time, the ductwork itself is holding residue that will reintroduce the smell every time the system runs, regardless of how well the rooms were treated.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

North Las Vegas summers push HVAC systems hard, and any odor sitting in ductwork gets redistributed every time the system cycles, which is why duct-aware treatment matters more here than in milder climates. The dry desert air also means porous materials dry out and seal odors in rather than letting them off-gas naturally, so smells that might fade on their own in a humid climate tend to persist longer here without treatment.

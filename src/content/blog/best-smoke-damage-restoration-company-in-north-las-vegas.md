## What Makes a Smoke Damage Restoration Company the Best Choice in North Las Vegas?

The strongest smoke damage companies share four traits: IICRC FSRT certification, 24/7 availability, in-house odor removal and contents cleaning, and a Nevada contractor's license that covers structural rebuild. Miss any one of these and you risk delays, a second contractor, or soot that gets sealed in instead of removed.

FSRT certification matters because smoke and soot behave differently than water damage. Dry smoke from a fast fire, wet smoke from a smoldering fire, and protein smoke from a kitchen grease fire each need a different cleaning approach and a different sealer before repainting. A technician without that training can wipe the visible soot and miss the acidic residue that keeps etching into metal, glass, and grout for weeks.

24/7 availability matters for a practical reason: soot residue is acidic and the longer it sits on finishes (chrome fixtures, electronics, painted trim) the more permanent the etching becomes. A company that only answers during business hours means more time for damage to set in overnight or over a weekend.

## What Questions Should You Ask a Smoke Damage Company Before You Sign?

Ask these five questions before you sign anything: are your technicians FSRT certified, will you give me a written scope before work starts, who handles odor removal and contents (your crew or a sub), what's your Nevada contractor license number, and how will you document the loss for my insurance adjuster?

A company that hesitates on the license number or can't name its certification is worth a second look elsewhere. Ask to see the license checked against the [Nevada State Contractors Board](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/) lookup tool directly; it takes thirty seconds and tells you whether the license is active and in good standing.

## How Long Does Smoke Damage Restoration Take?

Most smoke damage jobs in North Las Vegas take 3 to 10 days from assessment to final clearing, depending on how many rooms were affected and whether rebuild work (drywall, cabinets, flooring) is needed. A single-room grease fire with light soot might clear in three days; a whole-house smoke event after a structure fire, especially one requiring reconstruction, can run several weeks.

The timeline breaks into three phases: assessment and containment (day one), soot removal and odor treatment (days two through five, depending on scope), and final clearing with a post-treatment odor check before contents move back in. If rebuild work is part of the loss, that phase runs separately and is scoped like any other construction project. For a closer look at each phase, see [The Fire Damage Restoration Process, Explained](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/).

## Does Insurance Cover Smoke Damage in Nevada?

Most Nevada homeowners policies cover smoke damage when it results from a sudden, accidental fire, including the soot cleanup, odor treatment, and any resulting rebuild, minus your deductible. What's typically excluded is damage from a slow-burning or ongoing source the homeowner knew about and didn't address.

Document everything before cleanup starts: photos of every affected room, a list of damaged contents, and a written scope from the restoration company. That documentation is what an adjuster uses to approve the claim, and it's also what protects you if the insurer questions the extent of the loss later. If you have specific questions about your policy's smoke damage language, the [Nevada Division of Insurance](https://doi.nv.gov/) publishes consumer guidance on filing a claim.

**TL;DR:** If a pipe just burst, shut off your home's main water valve immediately, then cut power to any affected outlets if water is near them. Call a licensed plumber first to stop the source. Call a water damage restoration company at the same time, since drying needs to start within hours to prevent mold. Document everything with photos before cleanup begins for your insurance claim.

If you're reading this with water actively spreading across your floor in North Las Vegas right now, stop reading after this paragraph and go shut off your water. Burst pipes are common here when a sudden cold snap hits older copper lines, or when galvanized pipe in a pre-2000 home finally corrodes through. Whatever caused it, the next ten minutes matter more than anything else in this post.

## What do I do in the first 60 seconds after a pipe bursts?

Shut off the main water supply valve, then cut power to any outlets or fixtures standing in water. The main shutoff is usually near the water meter outside, in the garage, or under a utility sink. If you don't know where it is, shutting off the valve at the fixture closest to the break works as a temporary measure.

Once the water is off, move anything you can lift out of the water: rugs, electronics, furniture legs onto foil or blocks. Don't step into standing water near outlets or a breaker panel. If the water is rising near your electrical panel, leave the breaker alone and call the fire department's non-emergency line or your utility instead of touching it yourself.

Take photos of the water line on the walls and the source of the leak before you start moving anything else. This takes thirty seconds and matters more than it feels like it does right now.

## Who do I call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call both, within the same hour if you can. A licensed plumber is the only one who can stop the source for good, whether that's a split copper line, a failed fitting, or a cracked PEX connection behind a wall. A water damage restoration company handles everything the plumber doesn't: pulling standing water, drying the structure, and repairing what the water damaged.

The reason to call both right away, not one after the other, is timing. Drying needs to start within the first 24 to 48 hours, because that's the window before mold can start growing on wet drywall, insulation and framing, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). Waiting for the plumber to finish before you even think about drying can cost you that window.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after you call?

In the first 24 hours, a technician assesses the damage, extracts standing water, and sets drying equipment to begin pulling moisture out of the structure. Expect a moisture map of the affected rooms before any equipment goes in.

A typical sequence looks like this:

1. Moisture readings on walls, floors, and baseboards to map how far the water traveled.
2. Extraction of standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps.
3. Removal of materials that can't be dried in place, like soaked carpet pad or swollen baseboard.
4. Placement of air movers and a dehumidifier sized to the affected square footage, following the [IICRC S500 water damage standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/).
5. Daily moisture checks until readings return to a dry standard, which commonly takes three to five days depending on how much material got wet.

This is also the window to call your insurance company, after mitigation has started, not before. Most carriers want to see that you took reasonable steps to limit the damage.

## Will homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage like a burst pipe, but they exclude damage from gradual leaks, wear, or poor maintenance. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) outlines this distinction clearly: a pipe that suddenly splits is typically a covered peril; a slow drip that was ignored for months usually isn't.

Document the source of the break, the extent of the water, and keep receipts for anything you pay out of pocket before the adjuster arrives. A written drying log and moisture readings from your restoration company give the adjuster something concrete to work from, which tends to move the claim faster. For a deeper look at what's typically covered, see [our guide on homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

Once the pipe is fixed and drying is underway, the rest of the job is repair: drywall, flooring, paint, trim, whatever the water touched. That's a conversation for after the structure is dry, not before.

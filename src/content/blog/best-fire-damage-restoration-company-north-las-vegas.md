## What should you look for in a fire damage restoration company in North Las Vegas?

A qualified company holds a current Nevada State Contractors Board license in the classification that covers fire and smoke restoration work, plus IICRC certification specific to fire and smoke (FSRT), not just a general "restoration" badge. Those two credentials are the floor, not the whole decision.

Beyond licensing, look for a company that can document its process against the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/), the industry benchmark for content cleaning, structural cleaning, and deodorization after a fire. Ask whether the company's estimators are the same people who will be on site, and whether they provide a written scope before demolition starts, not after.

You can verify any contractor's license status directly through the [Nevada State Contractors Board](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/). A company that hesitates when you ask for its license number is not the company you want pulling permits on a rebuild.

## Does fire restoration include reconstruction?

Fire restoration and reconstruction are two distinct phases, but the best outcomes happen when one company handles both. Mitigation comes first: soot removal, smoke odor treatment, and drying out any water used to fight the fire. Reconstruction follows: framing, drywall, electrical, and finish work to put the structure back together.

Some companies only do the first half and hand you off to a general contractor for the rebuild, which means a second bidding process, a second contract, and a second set of delays while your insurance claim is already open. A company offering [fire damage restoration](/services/fire-damage-restoration/) alongside [reconstruction services](/services/reconstruction/) under one roof keeps the timeline, the paperwork, and the point of contact the same from day one through final walkthrough. Our [fire damage reconstruction cost guide](/blog/fire-damage-reconstruction-cost-in-north-las-vegas/) breaks down what that rebuild phase typically runs in North Las Vegas.

## What questions should you ask a fire restoration company before hiring?

Four questions tell you most of what you need to know in under five minutes on the phone. Ask for the license number and verify it yourself through the [Nevada State Contractors Board's consumer checklist](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/resources/consumer-checklist/), ask which IICRC certifications the crew actually holds, ask who will communicate directly with your insurance adjuster, and ask whether the HVAC system will be inspected and cleaned as part of the scope.

That last question matters more than most homeowners realize. Smoke odor rides through return air ducts and resettles in rooms the fire never touched. A company that skips duct inspection is leaving a known odor source in the house, which means you'll be back on the phone in three weeks wondering why the smell came back.

## What's different about fire and smoke damage restoration in North Las Vegas?

Desert climate changes how soot behaves once a fire is out. Dry heat and low humidity mean fine soot particulate stays airborne longer and settles into HVAC filters, evaporative cooler pads, and the block or stucco construction common in neighborhoods like Aliante and near Craig Ranch Regional Park. That particulate finds its way into attic insulation fast in homes with minimal roof ventilation, which is typical of the area's single-story tract housing.

Swamp coolers add a wrinkle most restoration crews outside the Southwest never deal with: a cooler pulling air through a sooty attic space can recirculate smoke odor through the whole house even after the fire damage itself looks cleaned up. A crew familiar with Las Vegas Valley housing stock checks the cooler and duct system as a matter of course, not as an afterthought.

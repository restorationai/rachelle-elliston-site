## How Much Does Fire Damage Restoration Cost in North Las Vegas?

Fire damage restoration costs in North Las Vegas typically range from $1,500 for a small, contained fire to $45,000 or more for a whole-house fire with heavy soot and odor. The biggest driver is how much of the home the smoke reached, not just where the flames were.

Smoke travels through HVAC ducts and gaps around doors far beyond the room that actually burned. A stovetop fire that never left the kitchen can still leave soot film on walls two rooms away, which is why a "small" fire sometimes costs more to clean than homeowners expect.

| Fire Size / Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Contained kitchen or appliance fire, odor only, no soot spread | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Single room, visible soot on walls and contents | $3,000 - $8,000 |
| Multi-room smoke and soot damage, moderate fire | $8,000 - $20,000 |
| Whole-house soot cleanup and content cleaning, major fire | $20,000 - $45,000 |
| HVAC duct decontamination and deep odor treatment (add-on) | $1,200 - $6,000 |
| Emergency board-up and roof/window tarping (standalone) | $300 - $1,500 |

These are industry-typical ranges, not a quote. Every fire loss is different, and a written scope of work should follow a site inspection before any cleanup begins.

## What Factors Affect Fire Damage Restoration Costs?

The main cost factors are the square footage affected by smoke, the type of materials that burned, and how quickly cleanup started after the fire was extinguished.

Plastics, synthetic carpet, and electronics produce denser, more corrosive smoke residue than wood or paper, and that residue is harder to remove from drywall and HVAC components. A fire that burned longer before suppression, or one where firefighting water soaked into subfloors and insulation, adds structural drying to the scope, which looks a lot like a standard water damage restoration job layered on top of the soot cleanup.

Other factors that move the number:

- **Square footage affected by smoke**, not just the room that burned
- **Protein fires** (grease, food) leave a sticky residue that needs specialized cleaners, not just wipe-down
- **Content volume**: how much furniture, clothing, and personal property needs cleaning, ozone treatment, or off-site pack-out
- **Odor severity**: light smoke odor may clear with air scrubbing; heavy odor often needs thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators run over several days
- **Access and timing**: fires reported quickly, with cleanup starting within 24-48 hours, usually cost less because soot hasn't had time to etch into surfaces

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Fire Damage Restoration?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies cover fire damage restoration, including smoke and soot cleanup, when the fire was sudden and accidental rather than the result of neglect. This typically falls under dwelling coverage for the structure and personal property coverage for contents.

Policies generally also cover additional living expenses if the home is uninhabitable during cleanup, per the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). What they don't automatically cover is damage tied to poor maintenance, like an electrical fire traced to wiring a homeowner knew was failing and never repaired.

Documentation matters for the claim. Photograph the damage before anything is moved, keep every receipt from the cleanup, and get a written scope of work rather than a verbal estimate. A restoration company that documents the loss properly, room by room, with moisture readings and photos, makes the adjuster's job easier and reduces disputes over what's covered.

## What Does the Fire Damage Restoration Process Involve?

The fire damage restoration process generally runs through five phases: assessment, board-up and water extraction, soot and smoke cleaning, content cleaning, and odor removal.

1. **Assessment and documentation.** A technician inspects every affected room, tests for lingering moisture from firefighting water, and documents the full scope for insurance.
2. **Board-up and tarping.** Broken windows, damaged doors, and compromised roof sections get secured the same day to stop weather and secure the property.
3. **Water extraction and drying.** Firefighting water gets extracted and structural drying equipment goes in, following the [ANSI/IICRC S700 standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/) for fire and smoke restoration.
4. **Soot and surface cleaning.** Walls, ceilings, and hard surfaces are cleaned with techniques matched to the soot type (dry sponge for light residue, solvent-based cleaners for protein or synthetic soot).
5. **Content cleaning and odor removal.** Salvageable furniture, clothing, and electronics are cleaned on-site or packed out; the final step uses air scrubbers, thermal fogging, or hydroxyl generators to neutralize odor at the molecular level rather than mask it.

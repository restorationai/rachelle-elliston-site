## What gets thrown away after smoke damage, and what can be saved?

Items that absorbed heat directly, or that are porous and perishable, usually get discarded rather than cleaned. Items with only surface soot, like hard furniture, most clothing, and electronics, can often be professionally cleaned and returned.

Plan to discard:

- Open food and anything in permeable packaging that was exposed to heat or smoke, since particulates and firefighting chemicals can contaminate it even when it looks fine.
- Prescription and over-the-counter medication that sat near heat, since elevated temperature changes the chemical stability of many drugs.
- Cosmetics, toiletries, and anything stored in soft plastic near the heat source. Melting and softening points are lower than most people expect.
- Baby formula and any already-opened food or drink containers.
- Items with visible structural heat damage: warped plastics, melted appliance housings, cracked glass or ceramics.

Usually salvageable with professional cleaning:

- Clothing and soft textiles, run through specialized wet cleaning or ultrasonic processes with enzyme-based detergents built for smoke odor.
- Solid wood and leather furniture with light surface soot, cleaned with HEPA vacuuming, targeted wipe-down, and a protective finish.
- Hard, non-porous items like glassware, metal fixtures, and most electronics, after a technician checks for internal soot or corrosion.
- Photographs and paper documents, which need careful, low-moisture handling rather than standard cleaning.

Metal items are worth moving fast on specifically. Soot residue is acidic, and in Southern Nevada's dry heat it can start pitting untreated metal within about a week.

## How does a contents pack-out and storage work?

A contents pack-out moves salvageable belongings out of a fire-damaged home so cleaning can happen while the structural rebuild is still underway, instead of one step waiting on the other.

1. **Inventory and photograph** every item before it leaves the house, itemized for the insurance claim.
2. **Sort on-site** into clean, cleanable, and non-salvageable categories.
3. **Pack and transport** salvageable items to a secure, climate-controlled facility.
4. **Clean off-site**: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, specialized wet or dry cleaning for textiles, and an [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/) process such as ozone or hydroxyl treatment for lingering smoke smell.
5. **Store items** until the structural [smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) and rebuild work is complete.
6. **Deliver and reinstall** belongings once the home passes final inspection.

This is also the part of the claim adjusters tend to ask about most closely, since contents coverage is a separate line item from dwelling repairs. The itemized, photographed inventory from step one is what supports that part of the payout. For what happens on the structural side during this same window, see [who to call after a house fire in North Las Vegas](/blog/who-to-call-after-a-house-fire-north-las-vegas/).

## Will insurance pay to clean belongings, or only to replace them?

Most homeowners policies cover both options under personal property coverage, and the adjuster typically approves whichever costs less: professionally cleaning and restoring an item, or paying its actual cash value to replace it. [Standard homeowners insurance](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) generally treats smoke and fire damage to personal property as a covered peril, subject to your policy's limits and deductible.

That cost comparison is exactly why the photographed inventory matters before anything leaves the house. An adjuster reviewing a damaged dining set, for example, needs to see its condition before cleaning to decide between a restoration estimate and a replacement payout. Items cleaned or discarded before documentation can be harder to claim.

If you're unsure whether a specific policy covers smoke-damaged contents separately from the home's structure, your agent or the adjuster assigned to the claim can confirm the personal property limit before pack-out starts.

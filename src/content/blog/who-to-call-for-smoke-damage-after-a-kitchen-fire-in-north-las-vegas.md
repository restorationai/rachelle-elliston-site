## Who should I call first for smoke damage after a kitchen fire?

Call the fire department first if you haven't already, even for a fire you put out yourself, so they can confirm there's no hidden hot spot in the cabinetry or wall cavity behind the stove. Once they clear the scene, call a smoke damage restoration company before you touch anything else.

Desert Valley Restoration is IICRC Certified and available 24/7 across North Las Vegas, Las Vegas, and Henderson for exactly this kind of call. A restoration crew assesses the smoke type, sets containment if needed, and starts cleaning before soot has time to etch permanently into painted surfaces, grout, and appliance finishes.

Your insurer comes after mitigation starts, not before. Most homeowners policies cover sudden fire and smoke damage, and documenting the damage with photos and a written scope from the restoration crew is what actually supports the claim, per the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). Calling the restoration company first, then looping in the adjuster with their documentation, is the order that keeps the claim moving.

## Is it safe to stay in the house after a small kitchen fire?

Usually yes for a contained stovetop or oven fire, but check for lingering smoke odor and soot on HVAC returns before deciding. If the smoke alarm went off for more than a few minutes or plastics/non-food items burned, airborne particulates can travel further than the visible damage suggests.

Run the kitchen range hood and open windows for cross ventilation if outdoor air quality allows, but don't run the central HVAC system until it's been checked. A running air handler pulls soot particles through the ductwork and spreads them into bedrooms and closets that never saw the fire. If anyone in the home has asthma or a respiratory condition, that's reason enough to have the air quality checked before settling back in, see the [EPA's guidance on indoor air quality](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq).

## What happens when you call a smoke damage restoration company?

The first call is a quick phone assessment, what burned, how long the alarm sounded, and whether it was food (protein smoke) or plastics and synthetics (dry smoke, heavier soot). That distinction changes the cleaning approach entirely.

Protein smoke residue, common with a grease or pan fire, is often nearly invisible, a thin, greasy film with a strong, sour odor that off-the-shelf cleaners can't cut. Dry smoke soot from burned plastic or wiring leaves visible black powder that smears if wiped with anything damp. A technician arrives with pH-balanced cleaning agents matched to the residue type, HEPA vacuums to lift loose soot before any wet cleaning touches a surface, and often a hydroxyl generator or ozone treatment to pull the odor out of soft materials like cabinetry, drywall, and textiles, following the [IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/).

Contents that absorbed odor (dish towels, upholstered seating near the kitchen, stored linens) typically get pulled for off-site ultrasonic cleaning and odor treatment rather than cleaned in place. That's its own workstream from the structural cleanup, and it's worth asking about up front if the smell has traveled into fabric and soft goods.

## Why can't I just wipe up the soot myself?

A damp rag on dry soot pushes carbon particles deeper into porous surfaces instead of lifting them off, which is why DIY cleanup often leaves a permanent gray shadow on paint and grout. The particles are microscopic and oily on protein smoke in particular; household cleaners smear it around rather than breaking it down.

The smell is the other half of the problem. Smoke odor isn't just sitting on the surface, it's absorbed into drywall paper, cabinet wood, and insulation. Painting over soot-stained cabinets or walls without sealing and deodorizing them first just traps the odor under fresh paint, and it typically resurfaces within a few weeks, especially in Las Vegas Valley heat when materials expand and off-gas. Professional odor removal treats the source, not just the smell in the air.

## What if the fire did more than kitchen smoke damage?

If flames spread beyond the stove, cabinets, or immediate wall surface, you're looking at structural fire damage, not just a smoke and odor job. That means charred framing, compromised drywall, or damaged electrical that needs inspection and rebuild, not just cleaning.

**TL;DR:** Fire damage reconstruction in North Las Vegas typically runs from $1,500 for smoke and soot cleanup alone up to $300,000 or more for a full structural rebuild after a near-total loss. A single room with structural damage usually falls between $8,000 and $25,000. Multi-room fires run $25,000 to $75,000, and fires that reach the roof or framing run $75,000 to $150,000. Most Nevada homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental fire damage under the dwelling coverage, minus the deductible, once the claim is documented and a written scope is in hand.

If the fire department just left and you're standing in a kitchen with a scorched wall, melted cabinet fronts, and soot settling into the hallway carpet, the question on your mind isn't how it started. It's what this is going to cost to put back together. The answer depends far more on how far the smoke traveled than on how big the flames looked. A small stovetop fire that fills a house with smoke can cost more to fully restore than a contained fire that stayed inside one cabinet.

## How Much Does Fire Damage Reconstruction Cost in North Las Vegas?

Fire damage reconstruction here typically costs between $1,500 and $300,000 or more, depending entirely on whether the job is cleanup only or a full rebuild from the slab up. There is no single per-square-foot number that applies across the board, because fire loss scope varies more than almost any other kind of damage a home can take.

| Scope of Damage | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Smoke and soot cleanup only, no structural rebuild | $1,500 - $5,000 |
| Single room with structural repair (drywall, framing, flooring) | $8,000 - $25,000 |
| Multiple rooms affected by fire and smoke | $25,000 - $75,000 |
| Major structural fire (roof, trusses, or load-bearing framing) | $75,000 - $150,000 |
| Near-total loss requiring full rebuild | $150,000 - $300,000+ |

These are typical industry ranges, not quotes. A [documented fire damage restoration](/services/fire-damage-restoration/) project starts with an in-person scope, because the same square footage can land at either end of a range depending on how deep the soot penetrated and what has to come out versus what can be cleaned.

## What Determines the Cost of Fire Damage Reconstruction?

Four things move the number most: how far the smoke traveled, whether the fire reached structural materials, how fast mitigation started, and what the city requires to rebuild to current code.

Square footage affected matters less than smoke penetration. Soot travels through HVAC returns and attic trusses far beyond the room where the fire started, which is common in North Las Vegas stucco-and-tile-roof homes in neighborhoods like Aliante and Eldorado, where attic ductwork runs the length of the house. A fire contained to one stovetop can still leave a film of soot in bedrooms two rooms away.

The type of fire matters too. A protein fire, meaning a kitchen fire fueled by cooking grease or food, leaves a thin, nearly invisible residue that carries a strong odor and is harder to fully neutralize than soot from burned wood or paper. That residue load drives odor remediation cost up even when the visible damage looks minor.

Timing matters as much as scope. Soot is acidic, and that chemistry works faster in the Mojave Desert's dry heat than it would in a humid climate. Left untreated even a few days, soot etches glass, pits metal fixtures, and permanently yellows grout and paint, turning a cleanable surface into one that has to be replaced.

Finally, any structural rebuild in North Las Vegas has to meet current building code, not the code the house was originally built under. That can mean upgraded electrical, smoke detectors, or egress requirements the original room didn't have. Permits for structural fire repair go through the [City of North Las Vegas permit application process](https://www.cityofnorthlasvegas.com/business/permit-application-center), and that review adds both time and cost that a cleanup-only job never sees.

## What Does Insurance Typically Cover for Fire Reconstruction in Nevada?

Most Nevada homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental fire damage under Coverage A (dwelling), paying for reconstruction up to the policy limit minus your deductible. Additional Living Expenses (ALE) coverage typically pays for temporary housing while the rebuild is underway, and most policies also cover smoke and soot cleanup to undamaged areas the fire didn't directly touch.

What usually is not covered: fires ruled intentional, damage tied to a vacant or unoccupied property beyond the policy's vacancy clause, and anything the adjuster deems unrelated to the covered event. The gap that trips up the most homeowners is the difference between what the adjuster's initial scope captures and what the actual rebuild requires, especially code-upgrade costs and hidden smoke penetration in the attic or wall cavities.

The claim moves faster when the pre-loss condition, square footage, and damage extent are documented in writing and in photos before any demolition happens. A licensed restoration contractor writes that scope the way an adjuster expects to see it, following the [IICRC's](https://www.iicrc.org/) S700 standard for fire and smoke restoration, which is often the difference between a claim that gets approved quickly and one that stalls over disputed line items.

## What Happens During Fire Damage Reconstruction?

A fire reconstruction job moves through roughly seven phases, from the first board-up to the final walkthrough.

1. **Emergency mitigation.** Boarding broken windows, tarping a compromised roof, and shutting off utilities if the fire department hasn't already, to stop further weather or security exposure.
2. **Assessment and documentation.** Moisture readings, soot testing, and a written scope of the damage, matched to insurance documentation requirements.
3. **Contents pack-out.** Salvageable belongings go to [off-site contents restoration and storage](/services/contents-restoration-storage/) for specialized cleaning while the structure is worked on.
4. **Demolition.** Removing drywall, flooring, and framing that soot or fire damaged beyond cleaning.
5. **Odor remediation.** Thermal fogging, ozone, or hydroxyl generators treat the structure for [smoke odor](/services/odor-removal/) that cleaning alone won't remove, particularly after a protein fire.
6. **Structural reconstruction.** Framing, drywall, electrical, and finishes rebuilt to current code under permit, covered under [general reconstruction services](/services/reconstruction/).
7. **Final clean and move-back inspection**, confirming odor, soot, and air quality are resolved before contents return.

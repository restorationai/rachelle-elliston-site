## Who do I call first after a house fire in North Las Vegas?

Call 911 first only if the fire isn't fully extinguished or someone needs medical attention. ## When do I need a restoration company vs. the fire department, utility company, or a plumber?

The fire department handles the fire itself and will tell you when it's safe to re-enter; everything after that, securing the structure and cleaning up the damage, is restoration work. A few situations call for a different number before anything else:

- **Gas smell or downed power lines:** call NV Energy or Southwest Gas immediately, and don't re-enter until they clear it.
- **The fire department red-tagged the structure:** don't attempt entry yourself, even to grab belongings. A restoration crew can coordinate safe, documented access.
- **Active water still running from a sprinkler head or burst line:** this is a restoration call, not a plumber's, because the water and the fire damage have to be mitigated together to avoid trapping moisture under soot-covered materials.
- **You need the structure boarded up overnight:** windows, doors, and roof openings left exposed invite weather and intrusion. This is typically the first physical task a restoration crew does on arrival.

Your insurance company doesn't need to inspect the property before mitigation starts. Most policies actually expect you to limit further damage right away. [The Insurance Information Institute's guide to settling claims after a disaster](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) confirms that documenting damage and taking reasonable steps to prevent it from getting worse protects your claim rather than complicating it.

## What happens when you call a restoration company after a fire?

The first call is a short phone intake: your address, whether the fire department has released the scene, and whether water from firefighting is still present. From there, a technician is dispatched to assess, board up openings, and start documenting damage for the insurance file.

On arrival, expect the crew to photograph every affected room before touching anything, since that documentation becomes part of your claim file. They'll board up broken windows and damaged doors, tarp any roof openings, and set up air scrubbers to start pulling soot particulate out of the air in rooms that didn't burn but still smell like smoke. If firefighting hoses left standing water, extraction starts the same visit, since wet drywall sitting under soot for more than a day or two becomes a much bigger teardown.

Have your policy number and insurer's claims line ready when you call. If you've already filed, having the claim number on hand speeds up the first visit. The [fire damage restoration process](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/) walks through what the full job looks like from that first assessment through final repairs, if you want the longer view before anyone arrives.

## Can you stay in your house after a fire?

Usually not right away, even if the fire was contained to one room. Smoke particulate travels through HVAC ducts and settles on surfaces throughout the home, and soot carries irritants that aren't safe to breathe in an unventilated space, especially for kids, older adults, or anyone with respiratory issues.

In a North Las Vegas neighborhood like Aliante or near Craig Ranch Regional Park, where many homes share similar stucco-and-tile construction with attached garages, a garage or kitchen fire can push smoke into the attic space and HVAC returns fast, even when the visible burn stayed small. A restoration tech can run air quality checks and tell you which rooms are livable and which need remediation before anyone moves back in. The [American Red Cross guide to recovering after a home fire](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/fire/home-fire-recovery.html) and local Red Cross chapters can also help with emergency lodging, clothing, and basic needs in the first 24 to 48 hours if your home isn't safe to occupy.

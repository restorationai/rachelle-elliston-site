In North Las Vegas, mold remediation typically costs $500 to $1,500 for a small, contained area like a bathroom or closet, and $3,500 to $15,000 or more for whole-house jobs involving multiple rooms, HVAC contamination, or structural drywall removal. The biggest factors are how much square footage is affected, whether the mold is hidden behind walls or under flooring, and whether porous materials need to be removed and replaced.

If you just pulled a baseboard off a wall near an AC closet and found black-green staining, or your inspector flagged a musty smell in a spare bedroom, you're probably trying to figure out whether this is a $600 fix or a five-figure rebuild. The honest answer: it depends almost entirely on what's behind the drywall, not on what you can see on the surface.

## What drives mold remediation cost in North Las Vegas?

The price is driven by four things: affected square footage, how many materials are porous and contaminated, how long the moisture source went unaddressed, and whether containment and negative air pressure are required to keep spores from spreading to the rest of the home.

In the Las Vegas valley, mold almost never shows up from ambient humidity the way it does in wetter climates. It follows a specific water event: a slab leak under a kitchen or bathroom, a clogged AC evaporator coil drain line dripping into a closet or attic space, a failed water heater, or monsoon rain finding its way through a roof penetration. Because the trigger is usually hidden (a slab leak under tile, a drain line behind a closet wall), the mold is often found weeks or months after the water event started, which means more material has had time to absorb moisture and support growth.

Square footage and material type matter more than most homeowners expect. Drywall and insulation are porous and almost always get cut out and replaced rather than cleaned. Hard surfaces like tile, concrete, and sealed wood can often be cleaned and treated in place, which keeps costs down.

## Does insurance cover mold remediation?

Most homeowners policies cover mold remediation only when the mold grew from a sudden, accidental covered loss, like a burst supply line, not from a slow leak or long-term maintenance issue. Many policies also cap mold coverage at a specific dollar amount regardless of the covered-loss rule, so check your declarations page for a mold or fungi limit.

A slab leak that's caught and reported quickly often qualifies as a covered water loss, with the resulting mold remediation included. A slow AC drain line leak that's been dripping for six months, discovered only when drywall starts to bubble, is a harder claim because insurers can argue it's a maintenance failure rather than a sudden event. Documentation matters here: photos of the source, the date you discovered it, and a prompt call to a restoration company all help support the claim. See the [Insurance Information Institute's guide to what standard homeowners policies cover](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) for the general rules insurers apply.

## What does professional mold remediation actually include?

Professional remediation follows a contain-remove-treat-verify sequence: isolate the affected area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure, remove contaminated porous materials, clean and treat remaining surfaces, then run air scrubbers and verify the area is clear before rebuild starts.

1. **Containment.** Plastic sheeting seals off the work area and a negative air machine keeps spores from migrating into clean parts of the house while work is underway.
2. **Removal.** Contaminated drywall, insulation, and flooring are cut back to clean material and bagged for disposal, following the practices in the [ANSI/IICRC S520 mold remediation standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/).
3. **Cleaning and treatment.** Remaining hard surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an antimicrobial to address what's left behind.
4. **Air scrubbing.** HEPA air scrubbers run for the duration of the job to pull airborne spores out of circulation.
5. **Clearance testing.** A third-party air or surface sample confirms spore counts are back to a normal range before drywall goes back up. Some jobs then move into drywall replacement and finish work, which is where [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) picks up.

## What should you do if you suspect mold right now?

Start with a professional inspection and air or surface sample before anyone touches the area, since disturbing mold without containment can spread spores into the rest of the house. A reliable DIY test kit from a hardware store can tell you mold is present, but it won't tell you the species, the extent behind the wall, or whether it's actively growing.

## Who do I call first when water is actively leaking?

If the water is still running, your first move is the shutoff valve, not the phone. Find the main water shutoff or the fixture's local valve and close it before you call anyone.

Once the water is stopped, call a restoration company, not a plumber, if the damage has already spread beyond the fixture itself: wet flooring, baseboards pulling away from drywall, water tracking into a hallway or under a wall from a bathroom near Aliante or a kitchen off Craig Ranch Regional Park. A plumber fixes what failed. A restoration crew handles everything the water touched after it failed, including extraction, drying equipment, and documenting the loss for your insurer.

If you can't locate or close the shutoff, or the leak is from a slab line you can't access, that's still a call to a restoration company first. Desert Valley Restoration's crews carry moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace water that's already migrated under tile or behind drywall, which is common in North Las Vegas homes built on slab foundations.

## When is it a 911 or utility call instead?

Call 911 or the gas/electric utility first if there's an active electrical hazard, a gas odor, or standing water anywhere near an electrical panel or outlet. No restoration crew should enter a space with a live electrical risk, and neither should you.

A sewer backup that's pushing sewage into living space (a Category 3 loss, meaning grossly contaminated water) is a call to a restoration company that handles sewage cleanup, like Desert Valley Restoration, rather than a general plumber. Keep people and pets out of the affected room until contamination is addressed; sewage contact carries real health risk and needs antimicrobial treatment under [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standards](https://iicrc.org/s500/).

If a city water main break or a widespread area flood is the source rather than something inside your home, that's a call to Las Vegas Valley Water District or the city, alongside your restoration call, since the drying and cleanup still falls on you as the property owner.

## When should I call my insurance company?

Call your insurer after you've stopped the water and started mitigation, not instead of it. Most policies require the homeowner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and documented, prompt mitigation protects the claim.

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst supply line or a failed appliance hose. They typically exclude gradual leaks, long-term seepage, and flood water from outside the home, and they only cover sewer backup or sump pump failure if you've added a water backup endorsement. Check your specific policy language; coverage varies by insurer and state. [The Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) has a plain-language breakdown of what standard homeowners policies typically cover.

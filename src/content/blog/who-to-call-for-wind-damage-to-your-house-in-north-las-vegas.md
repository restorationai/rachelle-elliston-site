## What do I do in the first few minutes after wind damage?

Scan for hazards before you touch anything: a downed power line, a gas smell, or a block wall leaning at an angle that could still fall. If you see a line down anywhere near the house or yard, stay back and call NV Energy, never attempt to move it or drive over it yourself.

If you smell gas or hear a hissing sound near a meter or appliance, get everyone outside and call 911 or your gas utility from a safe distance before calling anyone else. Once the scene is safe, photograph the damage from several angles before you move debris or start cleanup; insurers and contractors both rely on that first set of photos.

## Who do I call first, the restoration company or someone else?

Call the restoration or general contractor first when the damage is to the structure itself: a hole in the roofline, a blown-out window, missing stucco, or a downed patio cover or block wall. That's the call that gets plywood over an opening and a tarp on an exposed area the same day, before rain or another wind event turns a repair into a bigger loss.

Call your insurance company after the home is secured, not before. Filing the claim once mitigation is already underway, with photos and a written scope in hand, gives the adjuster a clear record instead of a verbal description of what happened. A contractor who also understands insurance documentation, which is part of what a company like Desert Valley Restoration handles day to day, can walk you through what the adjuster will ask for.

Only call a plumber separately if wind-driven rain pushed water into a wall or ceiling and you also have an active interior leak; otherwise the exterior repair and the interior drying can usually be handled under one scope. Reference our [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) coverage if a monsoon wind event pushed rain through a broken window or roof opening before it got covered.

## What happens when you call?

The first call is mostly intake: what's damaged, whether anyone is at risk, and whether water is actively getting into the house. From there, a crew gets dispatched to tarp a roof opening, board up a broken window or door, or fence off a hazardous section of a dropped block wall, usually the same day for an active intrusion.

Once the structure is secured, the scope moves to documentation and a written estimate: photos, measurements, and a list of what's damaged versus what's cosmetic. That written scope is what goes to the insurance adjuster and also becomes the basis for the rebuild, whether that's [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) work on siding and stucco or a larger [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) project if a wall or roof structure needs to be rebuilt.

## How do I know the contractor showing up is actually licensed?

Verify the license number on the [Nevada State Contractors Board](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/) website before you sign anything, especially if someone knocks on your door unsolicited right after a storm. Wind events in the valley reliably bring out storm chasers who go street to street offering fast repairs, cash deals, or pressure you to sign an assignment-of-benefits form on the spot.

A legitimate contractor's license number will match what's posted on the Board's site, and they won't need a large upfront cash payment to start. The Board's own [consumer checklist](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/resources/consumer-checklist/) is worth five minutes before you let anyone onto the roof or into the attic. If you want a longer rundown on vetting a company before a storm hits, see our guide on [how to choose a restoration company in North Las Vegas](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/).

## Will my homeowners insurance cover wind damage repairs?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden wind damage, such as a gust tearing off stucco or snapping a patio cover post, as a named peril, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance). Some policies carry a separate wind/hail deductible that's higher than your standard deductible, so it's worth checking your declarations page rather than assuming.

What usually isn't covered: damage from years of sun and wear on an aging patio cover or wall, since that falls under maintenance, not a sudden loss. Document the damage with photos before cleanup, keep any receipts for temporary board-up materials, and ask your contractor for an itemized written scope rather than a verbal quote. For a general breakdown of what a typical repair runs in this market, see our [storm damage restoration cost guide for North Las Vegas](/blog/storm-damage-restoration-cost-in-north-las-vegas/).

If the damage is limited to the roof covering itself, missing tile or shingles with no structural or wall damage, you'll also need a licensed roofer for that piece; a restoration and general contracting company secures the opening, handles structural and water-intrusion repairs, and manages the rebuild, but doesn't replace roof shingles or tile as a standalone service.

Get the structure secured first, get it documented in writing, and get a written scope before any repair work starts. That's the order that protects both the house and the insurance claim.

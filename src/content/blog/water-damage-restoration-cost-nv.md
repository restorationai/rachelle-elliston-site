## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in NV?

Most Nevada homeowners pay between $1,200 and $8,500 for standard water damage restoration, with severe losses (sewage, whole-home flooding, or long-standing water) running $10,000 to $25,000 or more. The final number depends on the water's contamination level, the square footage affected, and how many days passed before drying started.

Here's a realistic range by scenario, based on typical restoration industry pricing for the Las Vegas valley:

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Minor leak, single room, clean water (Category 1) | $1,200 - $3,000 |
| Moderate damage, 2-3 rooms, clean to gray water | $3,000 - $7,500 |
| Washing machine or dishwasher overflow (gray water, Category 2) | $4,000 - $8,500 |

| Whole-home flooding or slab saturation | $10,000 - $25,000+ |

## What Factors Change the Price?

The water's contamination category is the single biggest factor, followed by square footage, materials affected, and how long the water sat before anyone called for help. A Category 3 loss (sewage or flood water) always costs more than a Category 1 loss of the same size because contaminated materials often can't be dried, they have to be removed and replaced.

A few things that push the number up in Las Vegas valley homes specifically:

- **Slab foundations.** Many North Las Vegas homes sit on slab-on-grade construction, which means water that gets under the flooring can wick into drywall and baseboards across an entire room before it's visible. That extends drying time and often means more material removal than a homeowner expects.
- **Tile and stone flooring.** Common in desert homes, tile itself usually survives water exposure, but the thinset and subfloor underneath can trap moisture that needs specialized drying equipment to reach.
- **Response time.** Water sitting more than 48 hours in a dry desert climate can still support mold growth once it's trapped behind walls, even though the exterior humidity is low. A fast call keeps the job in the cheaper category.
- **Square footage and ceiling height.** Vaulted ceilings and open floor plans, common in newer Aliante and Craig Ranch-area builds, mean more cubic footage of air to dehumidify, which affects equipment count and drying time.
- **Code-required upgrades.** If drywall, insulation, or electrical needs replacement, current building code sometimes requires upgrades beyond a like-for-like repair, which adds to the reconstruction phase.

## Does Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration Costs in Nevada?

Most Nevada homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or a failed water heater, but exclude gradual leaks and flood water from the standard policy. If a pipe bursts overnight, that's typically covered. If a slow leak behind a wall went unnoticed for months, insurers often deny the claim as a maintenance issue.

Flood damage from rising water, including the flash flooding the Las Vegas valley sees during summer monsoon season, is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy entirely. Flood coverage requires a separate policy, often through the National Flood Insurance Program (see [FEMA's flood insurance program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance)). Check your policy's flood exclusion language before you assume you're covered.

The deductible is the real number that matters for small losses. If your policy has a $1,000 deductible and the repair comes in at $2,500, you're paying out of pocket for the difference on a cost-benefit basis anyway, and a claim still goes on your record. ## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Involve?

Water damage restoration follows a set sequence: stop the source, extract standing water, dry the structure to a verified moisture standard, then repair or rebuild what couldn't be saved. Skipping steps to save money almost always costs more later in mold remediation or repeat drywall replacement.

1. **Source control.** The water supply or appliance causing the leak gets shut off or repaired before anything else happens.
2. **Inspection and categorization.** A technician uses a moisture meter and thermal imaging to map how far the water traveled and assigns a water category (1, 2, or 3) per [IICRC S500 standards](https://www.iicrc.org).
3. **Extraction.** Standing water gets pulled with truck-mounted or portable extractors, usually within hours of the call.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run for 3 to 5 days typically, with daily moisture monitoring to confirm materials are drying, not just drying on the surface.
5. **Reconstruction.** Drywall, flooring, baseboards, and insulation that couldn't be saved get replaced to match the original finish.
